This release completes BLE CTS (Current Time Service: the time itself, the time zone and daylight saving, and whether the time can be trusted), replaces the hand-written UUID name table with one generated from official Bluetooth SIG data (from 9 characteristics to 512), and fixes three real problems: the DST offset was off by a whole step, "not given" time zones were encoded as concrete values, and connecting during a scan did not stop the scan.

### 🐞 Fixed

**The CTS daylight saving offset was off by a whole step** (found in a real-device demonstration)

- The specification expresses the DST offset in 15-minute units: `2` is half an hour of daylight saving, `4` is one hour and `8` is two hours. The old code was one step off across the board, treating `2` as one hour and `4` as two, and reported the valid value `8` as reserved.
- This kind of error is hard to notice: the time zone and the time are right, and only the daylight saving is half an hour out. It now follows the SIG specification with a regression anchor.

**Fields the device did not provide are no longer turned into concrete values**

- A time zone of `-128`, which the specification defines as "not given", used to become a definite `-32:00`, and a DST value of `0xFF` returned `0`, which is the conclusion "standard time". Such fields now return `null`, the raw bytes are preserved and `notes` explains why.
- Time accuracy `254` and `255` follow the same rule: out of range and unknown.

**Connecting during a scan stops the scan immediately**

- The scan used to keep refreshing the list every two seconds while connected, competing with the connection state and the service tree, and the adapter kept scanning for nothing. The scan now stops at the moment the connection succeeds, covering the button, the timers and the backend, and the data log records why it stopped.
- A race is fixed as well: if the backend reply to "start scan" arrived after a connection had already been made, it restarted scanning behind a button that read "start scan". Such a late reply now sends a stop to the backend.

**Characteristic names are left-aligned right after the UUID**

- The UUID column had a fixed width of 38 characters, so short UUIDs left a large gap and the name looked centred.

### ✨ New: the three BLE CTS characteristics (`0x1805`)

- **`2A2B` Current Time (10 bytes)**: `utc`, `dayOfWeekName`, `fractions256` and `adjustReasons`, with suspicious values called out, such as a year that looks like an uninitialised RTC, a weekday that does not match the date, and how far the clock has drifted, banded by minutes, hours and days.
- **`2A0F` Local Time Information (2 bytes)**: time zone and DST.
- **`2A14` Reference Time Information (4 bytes)**: time source (NTP, GPS, not synchronised), accuracy in eighths of a second, and days and hours since the last update. "Not synchronised", poor accuracy or 255 days without an update are direct evidence that the time reported by `2A2B` cannot be trusted.
- Fields are identified by length and never guessed. The `ble_cts_time` MCP tool takes HEX or a byte array.
- AI reading the log sees a readable interpretation: `ble_get_output` attaches `items[].decoded` and `decodedSummary` for CTS entries, so the HEX no longer has to be fed to a tool by hand.

### ✨ New: the UUID name table is generated from official data

- Service and characteristic names are generated from the official Bluetooth SIG assigned numbers: 78 services and 512 characteristics, including two non-SIG additions, with the fetch date and commit id at the top of the generated file.
- Visible changes: service names are more accurate (`0x1800` shows the SIG name GAP, `0x1801` shows GATT) and characteristic names grow from 9 to 512, for example `2A24` Model Number String and `2A26` Firmware Revision String.
- The behaviour "an unknown service is labelled `Custom Service`" is unchanged.
